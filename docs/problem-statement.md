# Problem Statement

## Who is affected?
Lecturers who set multiple-choice tests in higher education, and the students who sit them. Course coordinators and quality committees are also affected.

## What exactly is going wrong?
Test scores do not show whether a particular question was too easy, too hard, or unable to separate stronger students from weaker ones. Checking by hand means re-reading every response or building a spreadsheet for each quiz, so the check is often skipped.

## Why does it matter?
Poor questions can make marks unfair and hide the topics that students have not understood.

## Problem statement (3 to 5 sentences)
Lecturers who set multiple-choice tests in higher education, and the students who sit them, are affected when nobody knows whether each question worked as intended. Test scores show how well students did, but they do not show whether a particular question was too easy, too hard, or unable to separate stronger students from weaker ones, so weak questions are marked, graded and often reused without being noticed. In one published medical exam of 59 items taken by 45 students, 8 items (13.6%) did not discriminate between high and low scorers and 14 items (23.7%) were classed as difficult (Rezigalla et al., 2024). Checking questions by hand means re-reading every student's responses or building a spreadsheet for each quiz, which is slow when a lecturer teaches several courses, so the check is often skipped. This matters because poor questions can make marks unfair and hide the topics that students have not understood.

## Users and scope
| Stakeholder | Type | Interest |
|---|---|---|
| Lecturer | Primary user | Builds the question bank and quizzes; reads the analytics |
| Student | Primary user | Takes quizzes and sees MCQ marks |
| Course coordinator / quality committee | Stakeholder | Wants fair, consistent assessments |
| System administrator | Stakeholder | Manages accounts and deployment |
| FYP supervisor and examiner | Stakeholder | Evaluates the project |

In scope:MCQ question bank; online quizzes; automatic marking; Difficulty and Discrimination Index (CTT); visualisation dashboard; testing with simulated data.

Out of scope: essay marking; AI question generation or marking; proctoring or face recognition; machine-learning prediction; a full learning management system; deciding whether an answer key is correct (the system only flags it).
